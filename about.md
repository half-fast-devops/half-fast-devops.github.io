  <article class="zine-card torn">
    <h3>The Problem with 'DevOps'</h3>
    <p>Everyone's selling 'DevOps.' It's a job title, a certification, a six-step plan to digital nirvana that always seems to involve buying another tool. They're all Tyler Durden, whispering about rules, about projects, about how 'you are not your job.' They want to burn down the legacy monoliths and build their own perfect, prescriptive systems.</p>
    <p>They want to make you a space-monkey for their grand vision. But they miss the point.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="zine-card halftone">
    <h3>We Are All Marlena</h3>
    <p>Me? I'm Marlena Singer. I'm the tourist in the disaster. I'm the human variable, the little scratch on the roof of your mouth. I see the chaos, the failing pipelines, the 3 AM alerts, and I'm just here for the free coffee and to see what happens next.</p>
    <p>DevOps isn't a system you impose. It's the absurd, individual struggle. It's you, staring at a YAML file, rebelling against the meaningless void by insisting on two spaces instead of four. It's Sisyphus, but with a CI runner.</p>
  </article>

  <article class="zine-card">
    <h3>This Zine</h3>
    <p>This site isn't a guide. It's not a 'Best Practices' blog. It's a notebook. It's the scroll, rolled out on the floor of a car doing 90 down the digital highway. It's cut-and-paste thoughts, half-fast fixes, and notes from the road.</p>
    <p>It's about being human in a world of automation. It's about finding meaning in the terminal, even when the command fails. Don't panic. Just keep going.</p>
  </article>
</section>
