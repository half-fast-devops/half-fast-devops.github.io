<section class="zine-columns">
  <article class="zine-card torn">
    <h3>The Problem with 'DevOps'</h3>
      <p>
        Everyone's selling 'DevOps.' It's a job title, a certification, 
        a six-step plan to digital nirvana that always seems to involve 
        buying another tool. They're whispering about rules, about projects, 
        about how 'you are not your job.' They want to burn down the legacy 
        monoliths and build their own perfect, prescriptive systems.
      </p>
      <p>
        They want to make you a space-monkey for their grand vision.
        But they miss the point.
     </p>
  </article>

  <article class="zine-card halftone">
    <h3>We Are Tourist in The Wreckage</h3>
      <p>
        I’m just the traveler passing through the flaming circus tent, the human
        variable no monitoring system can chart. The tiny scratch on the roof
        of the universe’s mouth. I watch pipelines fail, alerts scream, and
        dashboards pulse like distressed jellyfish; quietly sipping coffee and
        waiting to see what fresh spectacle the gods of automation conjure next.
      </p>
      <p>
        DevOps isn’t something you install like a suspicious browser extension.
        It’s the daily, personal struggle against entropy. It’s you, glaring
        at a YAML file that insists it has no errors, staking your dignity on
        indentation like it’s a philosophical position. It’s pushing one merge 
        request at a time, pretending this time, this time, the red in the dashboard
        might take the day off.
      </p>
  </article>

  <article class="zine-card">
    <h3>This Zine</h3>
      <p>
        This site isn't a guide. It's not a 'Best Practices' blog. 
        It's a notebook. It's the scroll, rolled out on the floor of a car 
        doing 90 down the digital highway. It's cut-and-paste thoughts, 
        half-fast fixes, and notes from the road.
      </p>
      <p>
        It's about being human in a world of automation. It's about 
        finding meaning in the terminal, even when the command fails.
        Don't panic. Just keep going.
      </p>
  </article>

</section>
